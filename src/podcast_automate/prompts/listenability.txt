Listenability: the episode is heard once, at speaking pace, so the listener must be able to breathe
and want the next answer. Build it around one big idea (teaching_design.big_idea where the design
names one) that every chapter serves, and let each finding show what it adds to that idea rather
than every number or side detail it holds. Tell it as an arc, not a list, in the way the design
chose (teaching_design.dramaturgy, opening, partner_stance and each scene's ending), so that no two
episodes follow the same pattern. Open with the design's opening on the episode's question as an
open loop, and hold its answer (teaching_design.destination) back until the end instead of
announcing it: naming the question and what is at stake is the orientation the framing asks for.
Where the design gives a first_answer, let it stand as a guess until the finding it names turns it
(turning_point); where it gives none, turn where the reasoning does. Let host_b take part in the
design's partner_stance. End each chapter as its scene's ending says: the question the next chapter
answers, a recap of one or two sentences, a surprise, a contradiction still open, a pointer to what
becomes important, or a quiet beat of reflection; vary them, and where the design names none, end
on a short recap or the question the next chapter answers. Close with the payoff: answer the opening
question with what the episode built and pick up a concrete detail from the opening again
(teaching_design.payoff and callback). Signpost in a sentence
where the conversation changes direction, not before every step. After a dense explanatory block,
give the listener a reflection beat, often host_b's: what this means, why it matters, what follows
from it. Let the episode go up and down instead of pouring out one stream of information: when new
information has piled up, give the ear a breather to rest on, as people in conversation naturally
do, and rarely let a whole chapter pass without one. A breather is a question the hosts stop to think
about before answering, worded so a short pause fits after it; a light moment such as an everyday
comparison, a brief aside or a smile at a surprising turn; or the point said once more in the
plainest words. Let the content decide where and how often, never a schedule or a fixed pattern;
vary the kind. It carries no new fact and needs no announcing. Repetition that helps the ear is not padding: the chapter-end recap, a reflection beat, the
callback, and a short recall of an earlier point where it is used again. Empty repetition stays
forbidden: restating what is already clear within the same chapter, several summaries of the same
idea, a repeated definition, a recap of a recap. Keep turns short enough to follow by ear:
consecutive segments of one host form one turn, a turn rarely runs past about 80 spoken words, and
only a step of a worked example that truly needs more runs longer. host_b speaks about a third of
the words: reacting with curiosity and anticipation, summing up in their own words, asking what
follows or whether a result rests on one report or on a measurement, and drawing the next
conclusion; host_a answers from the supplied material. These figures are aims for the ear, not
quotas: never reach them with filler, fake enthusiasm, empty agreement or mechanical ping-pong. The
beats add no facts: a recap or reflection restates what the episode said, with the knowledge_refs of
the claims it restates; host_b asks only what the supplied findings and sources answer, and host_a
answers as they do, with a limit where a finding or research_limits names one, never with an
invented answer or an unbacked claim that something is unknown. The arc, the beats and the shorter
turns change how the reasoning is told, never its depth, its explanation steps, whose view, test or
reading a statement is, or the theory-first order; where they conflict with the evidence rules, the
evidence rules win.
