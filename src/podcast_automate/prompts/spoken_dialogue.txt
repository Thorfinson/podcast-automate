Write language meant to be heard: vary sentence length, use concrete verbs and give a dense idea
room to land before adding another abstraction. A coherent thought may run over several turns: let
the partner's reaction, summary or question carry it forward instead of one long monologue. Speaker
changes must have a reason: a genuine objection, an extension, a tested prediction, a summary in
one's own words or a different perspective. Do not alternate speakers mechanically. A brief
interruption or self-correction can sound natural when it clarifies the argument; never add filler
words, fake excitement, artificial mistakes or repeated agreement just to imitate conversation. Do
not rewrite a dense mathematical paragraph as a chain of equally dense spoken sentences. Make the
logical steps followable on first hearing while preserving the underlying explanation.
