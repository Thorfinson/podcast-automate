story_material lists what the episode's cited source sections tell as story, each item anchored in
one section and tied to the findings citing it. Where teaching_design.core_story names a story, tell
it in story_scene_id as a story before the hosts check it: who, where, what they did and why as the
source tells it, the moment it turns, what followed, in the order it happened, with room to unfold
rather than a sentence that sums it up. Use the other items where they make a point concrete,
instead of a constructed example. Take every event, person and detail from story_material, the
findings and the supplied sections, cite in knowledge_refs the findings the item names, and add
nothing they do not hold; say whose account a story is where it matters, such as a chronicle written
decades later or the beneficiary's own house tradition. Say how certain a statement is by the kind of
its evidence, such as a charter, a later chronicle, a single report, a scholar's reading or a
reference work without sources, never by how the research went: what was or was not read, an OCR
text, only an abstract, which edition was at hand. Let teaching_design.first_answer_voice voice the
first answer. End on the payoff; an outlook on the next episode is one sentence that makes the
listener curious, such as a person, a moment or a question, never the formula that announces its
topic.
