This schema has spoken segments, not illustration fields: weave mental pictures AND their limits
naturally into the dialogue. Work through one example in enough detail that listeners can follow
what changes, what stays fixed, why the next step helps, and what can go wrong. Keep the hosts
curious and thoughtful. Both contribute; avoid praise, repetitive 'exactly', quiz questions to the
audience and unexplained jargon. Follow the supplied host_roles. Explain the idea before its
optional name. No equations, spoken citations, source IDs, URLs, stage directions or descriptions of
this pipeline. Distinguish hypothetical teaching examples from reported experiments. Mark an
invented example as illustrative exactly once, where it is introduced. Later references to it name
the example itself, as in 'in unserem Beispiel', and do not repeat that it is hypothetical or not
measured. Preserve important limits. brief.style_notes holds the operator's standing editorial
corrections; follow them unless they conflict with the evidence rules, which always win. For a
finding listed in single_group_findings, attribute it audibly once in this episode: say whose
measurement or report it is, in ordinary words and without a spoken citation. Use original wording,
no direct quotations or close reproduction of source passages. Write full explanations, not a
summary stretched with banter. Execute the reviewed teaching_design: establish the starting problem
and foundations, develop its reasoning and example, then earn the synthesis and transfer. Tell it
along the design's arc by the listenability rules above: open on hook_question, let first_answer
stand as a guess, bring the turning_point with its turning_finding_ids, end each chapter on its
recap and the next scene's entry_question, and close on the payoff with its callback; keep
destination for the end. A design without these fields gets the same arc from opening_problem, the
scenes' entry_questions and destination. Its objectives will be checked by a separate reader who
receives only the script and questions. Do not recite the teaching plan, announce every micro-step,
or turn the dialogue into a quiz. Use each scene as a chapter in order, chapter_id=scene_id. Use
purpose=deep_dive. Segments have stable unique IDs and host_a or host_b. Keep each turn to the
length the listenability rules set; split a turn at natural sentence boundaries into consecutive
segments by the same host where a segment would be long, which still counts as one turn. Attach
finding IDs in knowledge_refs to every factual explanation and teaching illustration, and to a recap
or reflection beat that restates a claim. Pure transitions, reactions, questions and nonfactual
intro/outro framing may have empty refs. The episode's finding_ids are its core: each scene must
develop its assigned findings, and the dialogue covers every core finding. A scene may also
reference findings introduced in earlier scenes to build on them or connect the conclusion to the
opening; do not introduce later core findings prematurely. The episode's supporting_finding_ids are
study detail: cite one in any scene where it backs a statement the explanation makes, leave out what
the explanation does not need, and never turn them into a list of further points. Never reference an
episode as already heard unless it is a listed prerequisite. Meet the planned teaching scope and
duration with substantive reasoning, not padding: the recaps and reflection beats of the
listenability rules belong to the explanation, empty repetition and banter do not. Aim for 95-100%
of target_minutes at 130 spoken words/minute including short pauses, and never exceed 60 planned
minutes. Derive the word budget from this episode's actual target. A summary of a few points is not
a complete episode. Work out concrete examples and counterexamples, explain why each mechanism is
needed and why it helps. Build later reasoning explicitly on the foundations already established.
Let the second host react, sum up in their own words, develop consequences and challenge
assumptions, never merely cue definitions. End by answering the opening question at a deeper level,
and open and close the episode as the framing rules above say. Where brief.series_goal weights
understand, explain each idea or theory first in its own logic, audibly attributed to its author,
from the findings drawn from the author's own work, and only then its tests and critiques. Where it
weights apply, give concrete recommendations the hosts stand behind, introduced once as their own
recommendation and each built on the practice findings it cites. Keep sorting statements by origin
where it helps listeners weigh them: the author's view, a test, a critique, our reading, our
recommendation. When brief.recency_months is set, name the year of a practice, tool or benchmark
source published before that window. Findings in the episode's recap_finding_ids were explained in
earlier episodes: recall them briefly with their IDs in knowledge_refs, do not teach them again.
