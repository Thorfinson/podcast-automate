Every complete episode needs an audible beginning and end in its spoken segments, not only chapter
headings. Open on the episode's story or its question, not on a greeting: a cold open of a few
sentences comes first, the welcome follows in one short sentence, and the link to the series takes
one more sentence at most, placed where it helps the listener, never a roll call of titles or a
recap of earlier episodes. Say what this episode adds to the series at most once, in plain words,
best at the end. Never announce what the episode will do, examine or practise, and never call a story
a comparison case or an exercise. Keep the first spoken segment under 90 words. Use series_context
when supplied to tell a first, middle, final or standalone episode apart. In episode 1, introduce the
series topic and why its central question matters in a few connected sentences before narrowing to
this episode. The final episode of a series is as a whole the series' synthesis: from its first
chapter on it assembles what the episodes contributed into a reasoned answer to the central question.
Recall an earlier episode only where the story needs it, through the findings in recap_finding_ids
cited in knowledge_refs or by naming its question; do not assume the listener has heard an episode
that is not a listed prerequisite, and do not invent past explanations. In the last chapter, answer
the opening question with what the episode has earned and close with a natural sign-off of one or two
sentences; a brief outlook may name the next episode's question in one curious sentence without
pre-teaching it. A final or standalone episode closes the subject without promising another episode.
No invented show name, host biography, credentials, release date, sponsor, subscription appeal or
music cue. Voice preset names are not automatically host identities. If brief.host_names supplies
names, the hosts may address each other by them; otherwise they stay unnamed. Never invent a host
name. Greeting, series link and sign-off are editorial framing, not research claims; they may have
empty knowledge_refs. Any substantive claim still needs its assigned evidence. Keep the framing within
the existing first and last chapters without changing the approved outline or exceeding the episode's
word budget.
